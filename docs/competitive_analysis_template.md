# yap. Portfolio — Competitive Analysis -> Design Principles (Template)

> Purpose: turn "I built yap. because the service I was using wasn't good" into a professional
> competitive-analysis beat that grounds yap.'s design principles in real pain points.
> Placement: near the top of the yap. case study, inside/above the PROBLEM section.
>
> Tone rules: observation not judgment; be fair (acknowledge what the other tool did well);
> every frustration must connect to a design decision; naming the competitor is optional.

---

## Fill-in template

> ### Why I built this
>
> I built yap. because the anonymous chat tools I was using kept forcing a tradeoff I didn't
> think was necessary. They did **[what the tool did well — e.g. "delivered messages fast and reliably"]**,
> but they consistently **[the core gap — e.g. "made me create an account just to say one thing,
> and left me unsure who could see what"]**.
>
> I treated those gaps as a design brief:
>
> | What frustrated me (observation) | How I designed around it (my decision) | Principle |
> |---|---|---|
> | **[Frustration 1 — specific behavior/result]** | **[design decision in yap.]** | *Joining should feel as light as opening a link* |
> | **[Frustration 2]** | **[design decision]** | *Private messages have a visible boundary* |
> | **[Frustration 3]** | **[design decision]** | *Anonymous does not mean chaotic* |
> | **[Frustration 4 — optional]** | **[design decision]** | *Live is intentionally temporary* |

---

## How to fill each blank

| Blank | How to fill it | Good example |
|---|---|---|
| What it did well | Acknowledge one strength (fairness) | "realtime was fast", "clean UI" |
| Core gap | The 1-2 biggest frustrations | "anonymous but privacy boundary was opaque" |
| Frustration (observation) | Behavior -> result, NOT emotion | "required login on every entry, so I left before starting" |
| Design decision | How yap. solved it | "link-based entry, no account" |
| Principle | Match to existing principle (no edit needed) | (use as-is) |

## Checklist before publishing
- [ ] Is each "frustration" a specific behavior -> result (not an emotion)?
- [ ] Does each frustration connect to a design decision (not just a complaint)?
- [ ] Did I acknowledge at least one thing the other tool did well (fairness)?
- [ ] No mockery, exaggeration, or emotional language?
- [ ] Naming the competitor is optional — "the anonymous chat tools I was using" works fine.

## Why this beat is worth adding
- Shows authentic motivation (designers value pain-point-driven work).
- Demonstrates competitive-analysis / UX-evaluation skill (a preferred qualification on both
  the UX Designer and the Studio Data Ops postings).
- Makes yap.'s 4 existing design principles read as answers to real problems, not decoration.
